The problem

Cheap only matters if you’re comparing the same game.

Marketplace searches mix original releases, reissues, demos, imports, incomplete copies and different regional versions.

Wrong region

PAL, NTSC-U and NTSC-J copies belong in different markets.

Wrong edition

Original, budget, promo and special editions can have very different collector values.

Wrong completeness

A complete copy should not be valued against disc-only, no-manual or case-only listings.

How it works

Classify first. Compare second.

01Find listings

Use authorised marketplace inventory sources.

02Identify release

Resolve platform, region, territory and edition.

03Check completeness

Separate complete copies from incomplete or misleading listings.

04Compare properly

Only equivalent copies enter the same price bucket.

Accuracy comes before coverage. RetroNomad is designed to return “needs review” when evidence is weak instead of pretending every listing can be classified with certainty.

FAQ

Built for collectors, not just price searching.

Is RetroNomad another collection tracker?

No. The launch product is focused on helping buyers identify and evaluate listings.

Does “comparison-ready” mean “good deal”?

No. It means the listing has enough evidence to enter the correct comparison bucket. Deal grading comes only after trustworthy reference pricing.

Will it support US and Japanese games?

That is the intention. RetroNomad is the global product; PALScout is simply the first regional identification layer.

Is RetroNomad affiliated with eBay or console manufacturers?

No. RetroNomad is an independent project. Future marketplace integrations will use authorised or otherwise permitted access.
